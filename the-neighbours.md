# The Neighbours

Outcome: you can say, for the tools a
reader is most likely choosing between,
what each does that Vikunja doesn't —
and what Vikunja does back.

**Todoist** (cloud) is the polished
personal list: superb quick capture,
natural-language dates ("water plants
every Sunday"), and apps everywhere. It
is also a subscription for its reminders
and filters, your data lives with the
company, and there is no server of yours
in the story at all. Vikunja answers with
ownership and projects-with-boards;
Todoist answers with an input box that
feels like magic. If your lists are
personal and many, its pull is real.

**Trello** (cloud) defined the kanban
board for most people, and boards are all
it is: no real list or Gantt view, dates
as decoration, and automation as the
paid-tier prize. Vikunja's four views of
the same tasks are the direct reply.
Trello's counter is a decade of polish,
power-ups and everyone already knowing
it.

**Microsoft To Do / Google Tasks**
(cloud) are the free lists bundled with
accounts you already have. Fine for
shopping; neither pretends to hold a
project with stages, sharing levels and
an API you may script. They are what
Vikunja is not trying to be.

**Nextcloud Deck + Tasks** (self-hosted)
live inside the Nextcloud suite this
series covers in the
[Nextcloud guide](https://paulsullivan33-dev.github.io/nextcloud-for-mere-mortals/):
boards in the files-and-calendar house,
tasks through the same CalDAV world as
chapter 10. If your household already
runs Nextcloud, its deck may be
kanban enough; Vikunja is the deeper
task manager (views, filters, tokens),
Nextcloud is the wider house.

**Wekan and Planka** (self-hosted) are
boards first — close Trello relatives
under your own roof — and they matter
here twice over: they are the neighbours,
and they are two of the five importers
chapter 12 found in this build, which
tells you the direction users usually
walk between them and Vikunja.

**Plane and Taiga** (self-hosted) are the
heavier end: sprints, epics, story
points, issue tracking — software-team
ceremony Vikunja deliberately lacks. If
your work has a product owner, you want
these; if it has a shed, you don't.

**A spreadsheet, or
[NocoDB](https://paulsullivan33-dev.github.io/nocodb-for-mere-mortals/)**
(self-hosted) remains the quiet rival:
many "projects" are really tables people
glance at. If nobody needs reminding and
the board is never looked at, a table is
honest tooling. Vikunja earns its place
the moment dates, assignees and a *Doing*
column start mattering.

The pattern to keep: cloud tools sell
polish and presence everywhere; Vikunja
sells the deed to the building. Its four
views, its calendar door and its scriptable
API are the rooms that deed buys.
